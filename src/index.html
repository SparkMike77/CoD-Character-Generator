Instructions: Allocate the 5 / 4 / 3 points by horizontal row, not by vertical column.

The three rows are the Attribute categories:

Power: Intelligence, Strength, Presence
Finesse: Wits, Dexterity, Manipulation
Resistance: Resolve, Stamina, Composure

Choose which row is most important to your character:

Primary row: add 5 dots
Secondary row: add 4 dots
Tertiary row: add 3 dots

Skills range from 0 to 5 dots:

0 dots: No meaningful training; you can often still try, but you take an unskilled penalty
1 dot: Basic familiarity, hobbyist knowledge, or introductory training.
2 dots: Professional competence.
3 dots: Highly skilled or notably experienced.
4 dots: Expert.
5 dots: Among the best in a field.

A Specialty is a narrow focus within a Skill that adds +1 die when it applies. For example, someone with Medicine 2 and Medicine (First Aid) effectively rolls as though they have Medicine 3 for CPR, stabilizing injuries, or applying a pressure bandage.

Earning Beats

You usually earn a Beat when one of these happens:

You fulfill an Aspiration. Replace that Aspiration at the end of the session (or when appropriate) with a new one.

You resolve a Condition according to its listed resolution requirement.

You choose to turn an ordinary failed roll into a dramatic failure—the roll fails badly and creates a meaningful complication.

You roll a dramatic failure on a chance die.

A Breaking Point occurs: your character faces something that challenges their Integrity, morality, or sense of self.

The Storyteller may award Beats for significant story developments, especially when a character’s choices create real complications.

Using Beats

Every 5 Beats become 1 Experience: when the fifth box fills, clear the track and add 1 to Experience. Experience buys new dots — see the Experience costs.

Group Beats

Group Beats are earned by the group as a whole when the group’s shared goals are met. Every 5 Group Beats give each member of the group 1 Experience; then the track clears.

Health

Health is how much physical punishment your character can take: one box per point, equal to Stamina + Size.

Each point of damage fills a box. When every box is filled, your character is taken out — knocked out, severely injured, or dead, depending on the kind of damage and the Storyteller’s ruling.

Lethal damage, which most weapons deal, is more serious than bashing damage. Serious injuries can also bring Conditions such as Arm Wrack, Leg Wrack, or Ongoing Injury.

Integrity

Integrity measures your character’s moral and psychological stability — not how good they are, but how well they hold together. Mortal characters start at 7.

Horror, trauma, betrayal, violence, and choices that violate the character’s sense of self are Breaking Points. Define a few personal ones when you build the character. When one occurs, the Storyteller calls for an Integrity roll; failure can reduce Integrity or impose a Condition.

Facing a Breaking Point also earns a Beat. Integrity can be raised with Experience (2 per dot).

Tracked Resource

Swarm pool size: your Swarm pool starts at 1 when your character earns their first Aptitude dot (a Protocol dot, in Apocalypse), and grows by 1 with each dot after that, up to a maximum of Stamina + Manipulation.

Each species in your Chronicle can have its own resource pool — for example Blood Pool for Vampires, Rage for Werewolves, Swarm for the Changed, or Luck for Humans. It fuels that species’ abilities: the species’ rules say what spends points, how they’re regained, and any limit on how many you can hold.

Mark the points your character currently has. The details for your species are in the Game Setting and Campaign Rules tabs.

Features

This list holds your species’ supernatural traits — Disciplines for Vampires, Gifts for Werewolves, Aptitudes for the Changed — each rated in dots. Your species’ rules, in the Game Setting and Campaign Rules tabs, say what each one does and what it costs.

In Modern, the Changed have five Aptitudes: Making, Shaping, Animation, Lightning and Sensing. Each dot unlocks that Aptitude’s abilities at that level; the first dot also gives its free Basic Trait. In Apocalypse, a character belongs to one Aptitude and lists its Protocols here instead.

Aptitudes and Swarm: your Swarm pool starts at 1 when your character earns their first dot in this list, and grows by 1 with each dot after that, up to a maximum of Stamina + Manipulation.

Virtue

Your character’s Virtue is their higher calling: the trait that shows them at their best. Acting on it is hard, and usually costs or risks something.

Acting on your Virtue in a way that matters is how your character regains the most Willpower: all of it, once per chapter.

Virtues are open-ended. Pick a suggestion from the list or write your own: a word for the trait, plus the kind of moment that triggers it, agreed with the Storyteller. Examples from the rules:

  • Competitive: honestly competing with someone who outclasses them, for stakes they’d feel if they lost.
  • Generous: being deprived of something important, or put at risk, because of their generosity.
  • Just: setting their own goals aside to make sure justice is done.
  • Loyal: refusing to act against the group’s interests when it puts them at risk.

Many tables still use the seven classic Virtues of the original World of Darkness: Charity, Faith, Fortitude, Hope, Justice, Prudence and Temperance.

A character can’t have the same trait as both Virtue and Vice. Either can change after a life-altering event.

Vice

Your character’s Vice is their short-term comfort: the easy way out. It’s self-destructive in the long run, but it relieves stress.

Indulging your Vice regains one Willpower point, once per scene.

Vices are open-ended. Pick a suggestion from the list or write your own: a word for the trait, plus the kind of moment that triggers it, agreed with the Storyteller. Examples from the rules:

  • Ambitious: jockeying for position or advancing socially instead of doing something more useful.
  • Arrogant: taking an opportunity to lord their superiority over someone.
  • Competitive: indulging in a competition that distracts from more pressing matters.
  • Greedy: taking an advantage for themselves and denying it to someone else.

Many tables still use the seven classic Vices of the original World of Darkness: Envy, Gluttony, Greed, Lust, Pride, Sloth and Wrath.

A character can’t have the same trait as both Virtue and Vice. Either can change after a life-altering event.

Aspirations

Aspirations are short statements of what your character wants to accomplish — or what you, as a player, want to see happen to them. They are active goals that give the Storyteller story hooks to build scenes around.

Keep three at a time, with a mix of short-term and long-term goals.

Fulfilling an Aspiration earns a Beat; replace it with a new one at the end of the session, or when appropriate. One that no longer fits the story can be swapped between chapters with Storyteller approval — for changed circumstances, not to dodge a difficult goal. Getting Started has examples.

Conditions

Conditions are temporary states that change what your character can do — for example Knocked Down, Stunned, Arm Wrack, Blinded, or Ongoing Injury. They come from combat and injuries, failed Integrity rolls at Breaking Points, powers, and the story.

Pick each Condition your character has from a dropdown; its effect, with the dice penalties, shows underneath. Many describe what resolves them; resolving one that way removes it and earns a Beat.

The sheet applies what it can: Defense, Speed and Initiative change (in red, with the reason below them), and injured body parts show as Damaged on the avatar. Penalties to particular kinds of roll, such as -3 to sight-based rolls, are yours to apply when you roll.

The full list used at this table is on the Combat Rules tab.

What this is and what this is not.

This is a character builder and manager that's largely compatible with CoD Hunter: The Vigil. It grew out of a combination of a really excellent home game group and wildly unmanaged ADHD at the table. Exceptional numbers of character sheets were lost, StoryTellers were disappointed and time was wasted. In order to reduce the blood-pressure of the StoryTeller I created this ...thing.

First and foremost, I strongly recommend buying the books to keep food on their tables and your brain from exploding. Truly, it's a pain to clean up and nobody wants that. I bought my copies from DriveThruRPGs, but your mileage may vary and I love me some PDFs. Start with Hunter: The Vigil (second edition). It's beautifully dark, moody and full of gorgeous art. It's playable standalone and you can bolt on the other books or fancy bits from the CoD main book.

Second, While this app is standalone it works a lot better if paired up with the rest of the ecosystem, GMScreen and Character Manager (or lots of Character Managers each on a different player device) connect together to allow the StoryTeller to directly see character details. No more asking Joe "Hey, what's your health at?" from across the table. StoryTellers can create "Campaign Packs" that use an existing (or homebrew!) setting with added information, locations, tips, etc. It's all backed by Markdown files (.MD) that get passed from GMScreen to Character Manager as needed. Both apps are free to use, and I'll incorporate any ideas that sound clever.

In spite of (and largely because of) how much I like the art style in the CoD books I will not include or ape it here. That includes pictures, logos, lore, lunch orders or plot-lines. Artists' work is how they eat and I would not dream of stealing their snacks. This app is going to be boring and ugly by comparison. Seriously, buy their books. Support your local game store (if you have one).
