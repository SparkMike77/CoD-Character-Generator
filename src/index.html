Instructions: Allocate the 5 / 4 / 3 points by horizontal row, not by vertical column.

The three rows are the Attribute categories:

Power: Intelligence, Strength, Presence
Finesse: Wits, Dexterity, Manipulation
Resistance: Resolve, Stamina, Composure

Choose which row is most important to your character:

Primary row: add 5 dots
Secondary row: add 4 dots
Tertiary row: add 3 dots

Skills range from 0 to 5 dots:

0 dots: No meaningful training; you can often still try, but you take an unskilled penalty
1 dot: Basic familiarity, hobbyist knowledge, or introductory training.
2 dots: Professional competence.
3 dots: Highly skilled or notably experienced.
4 dots: Expert.
5 dots: Among the best in a field.

A Specialty is a narrow focus within a Skill that adds +1 die when it applies. For example, someone with Medicine 2 and Medicine (First Aid) effectively rolls as though they have Medicine 3 for CPR, stabilizing injuries, or applying a pressure bandage.

Earning Beats

You usually earn a Beat when one of these happens:

You fulfill an Aspiration. Replace that Aspiration at the end of the session (or when appropriate) with a new one.

You resolve a Condition according to its listed resolution requirement.

You choose to turn an ordinary failed roll into a dramatic failure—the roll fails badly and creates a meaningful complication.

You roll a dramatic failure on a chance die.

A Breaking Point occurs: your character faces something that challenges their Integrity, morality, or sense of self.

The Storyteller may award Beats for significant story developments, especially when a character’s choices create real complications.

What this is and what this is not.

This is a character builder and manager that's largely compatible with CoD Hunter: The Vigil. It grew out of a combination of a really excellent home game group and wildly unmanaged ADHD at the table. Exceptional numbers of character sheets were lost, StoryTellers were disappointed and time was wasted. In order to reduce the blood-pressure of the StoryTeller I created this ...thing.

First and foremost, I strongly recommend buying the books to keep food on their tables and your brain from exploding. Truly, it's a pain to clean up and nobody wants that. I bought my copies from DriveThruRPGs, but your mileage may vary and I love me some PDFs. Start with Hunter: The Vigil (second edition). It's beautifully dark, moody and full of gorgeous art. It's playable standalone and you can bolt on the other books or fancy bits from the CoD main book.

Second, While this app is standalone it works a lot better if paired up with the rest of the ecosystem, GMScreen and Character Manager (or lots of Character Managers each on a different player device) connect together to allow the StoryTeller to directly see character details. No more asking Joe "Hey, what's your health at?" from across the table. StoryTellers can create "Campaign Packs" that use an existing (or homebrew!) setting with added information, locations, tips, etc. It's all backed by Markdown files (.MD) that get passed from GMScreen to Character Manager as needed. Both apps are free to use, and I'll incorporate any ideas that sound clever.

In spite of (and largely because of) how much I like the art style in the CoD books I will not include or ape it here. That includes pictures, logos, lore, lunch orders or plot-lines. Artists' work is how they eat and I would not dream of stealing their snacks. This app is going to be boring and ugly by comparison. Seriously, buy their books. Support your local game store (if you have one).
